You will always know what you are paying for.

The AI tutoring platforms are right about one thing: parents should see progress, not hope for it. The difference here is that a person reads the data, teaches from it, and tells you what it means.

Paper score log

Every past paper is sat under timed conditions, marked, and logged. The trend is visible paper by paper and topic by topic, so nobody is guessing what grade is coming.

Mark-loss breakdown

Each marked paper is broken down by why the marks went: misread question, wrong method, arithmetic slip, or ran out of time. We fix the cause, not the symptom.

Post-lesson summary

A short WhatsApp message to parents after every lesson: what was covered, what changed on the tracker, and what has been set for the week.

A month planned in advance

Lessons and their topics are scheduled a month ahead, with timed papers placed where they will tell us most. Parents can see the plan, not just the next booking.

Help between lessons

Stuck on a homework question on a Wednesday night? Students can send a photo of it and get a reply the same day, not a wait until the next lesson.

Monthly progress report

One page, once a month: the tracker, the paper trend, what has moved, and what the next four lessons will focus on. Written in plain English, not dashboard-speak.

Software can log a score. It cannot notice that a student rushes every question with a diagram, or that the Further Maths gap is really an A-level Maths gap in disguise. That is what the lessons are for.

Getting into the right university.

For Year 12 and 13 students applying for Maths, Economics, Finance and related courses. The application and the admissions tests are separate problems, and both are taught.

UCAS and the personal statement

The personal statement is now three set questions with 4,000 characters shared between them and a minimum of 350 per answer. It rewards specific evidence and punishes filler, which is where most applicants fall down.

  • Course and university choiceRealistic targets, one aspirational choice, and a sensible insurance
  • The three questionsWhat to put where, how to split the characters, and what admissions tutors are looking for
  • Drafting and editingLine-by-line feedback across as many drafts as it takes
  • Super-curricular materialReading, competitions and projects that give you something worth writing about

TMUA, MAT and STEP

Admissions tests sit well above A-level and are the deciding factor for the most competitive Maths and Economics courses. They are learnable, but not by doing more A-level questions.

  • TMUAI sat it myself and scored above 7 out of 9. Used by Cambridge Economics and a growing list of Maths and Economics courses, including LSE, Warwick and Durham
  • MATOxford and Imperial Mathematics and joint honours. Multiple-choice and longer problems that reward clean thinking over speed
  • STEPCambridge and Warwick offers, sat in June alongside A-levels. The hardest of the three and the closest to university mathematics
  • Timed practice, markedFull past papers under conditions, marked with feedback, and scores logged the same way as everything else

I went through UCAS for Mathematics myself and sat the TMUA on the way in. For MAT and STEP, three years of university mathematics at UCL means the style of question is familiar ground: problems that need an idea, not a method from a textbook. I have also been on the other side of competitive applications in finance, so the advice comes from having done it.
